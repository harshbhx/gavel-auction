# Multi-day simulation of the settlement flow

This file is generated by `packages/payments/test/simdays/multiday.test.ts` (CI job `sim-multiday`, workflow `.github/workflows/sim-multiday.yml`). Do not edit it by hand: run the job with `SIM_MULTIDAY_WRITE=1` and commit the result.

## What this proves and what it does not

- It runs OUR code (settlement saga, reauthorization pass, journal stores, webhook merge) over simulated days against a MODEL of PayPal's authorization rules, in seconds, with an injected clock.
- The model is the fake PayPal in `packages/payments/src/fake/` (assumptions A07 to A11 in `ASSUMPTIONS.md`): honor period 3 days, authorization valid 29 days, reauthorize allowed only after the honor period and answered with a new authorization id, capture after validity fails. The strict reading is used: a capture after the honor period is refused (`afterHonorCapture: fail`). Scenario 3 also shows the lenient reading.
- It does NOT replace the real G3 probe. A passing run says our code behaves correctly if PayPal follows this model. It says nothing about whether PayPal does.
- Real G3 (sandbox probe, 5 real days) will confirm or correct: whether an authorization stays capturable after 3 days, the exact error name for a capture after the honor period, whether reauthorize is refused inside the honor period and with what name, whether reauthorize returns a new id and closes the old one, whether more than one reauthorization is allowed, and whether PayPal sends a webhook when an authorization expires. Where G3 disagrees, change the fake (and `ASSUMPTIONS.md`) and rerun this suite.
- Process crashes are simulated by an exception at a saga boundary. In-process exceptions release the settlement lease on the way out, so the restart waits out the lease time instead of losing it; scenario 6 reads the journal back from a file after each crash.

## S8: 30 simulated days, 100 bidders, random crashes

- day 0 to 30: 40 lots, 100 bidders, 3% crash chance at every boundary, outages of 1 to 4 days in 5% of crashes, random PayPal faults; every fifth lot (8 lots) gets two injected 429s on its first capture and a restart after a 2-hour outage, past the unknown deadline
- the 429 bug is fixed (https://github.com/harshbhx/gavel-auction/issues/1937, PR https://github.com/harshbhx/gavel-auction/pull/1938): a 429 starts no unknown clock, and 0 lots in this run reached review because of one. A timeout, a 5xx or a lost answer followed by an outage longer than the unknown deadline still reaches review by design: 0 of the 0 lots in review began that way. 0 lots began with a definite refusal (not a 429 and not an ambiguous outcome): PayPal refused the capture. A refusal that names the hold (declined, expired, voided, or a capture past the honor period) falls back to the runner-up at once, also after a 429 stall (issue https://github.com/harshbhx/gavel-auction/issues/1946); any other refusal keeps the lot under the unknown deadline. The cause of each lot in review is listed in the measures.
