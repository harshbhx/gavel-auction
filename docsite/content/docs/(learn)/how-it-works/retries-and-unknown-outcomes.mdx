A capture call can end in four very different ways, and charging the wrong person depends on telling them apart.

<Cards>
  <Card title="Taken" description="PayPal answers COMPLETED. Money moved. The settlement goes on." />
  <Card title="Refused" description="PayPal says no and says why (declined, expired). Nothing moved. Safe to try the runner-up, once the truth confirms it." />
  <Card title="Not run" description="HTTP 429. PayPal refused to run the request. Nothing moved and nothing is in doubt. Wait and repeat the same call." />
  <Card title="Unknown" description="A timeout, a network failure or a 5xx. The capture may or may not have happened. Never charge the runner-up on a maybe." />
</Cards>

## The order of the checks

<Steps>
<Step>

### The transport retries first

Network errors and the statuses 429, 500, 502, 503 and 504 are retried inside one request: at most four attempts of ten seconds each, full-jitter backoff from 250 ms up to four seconds, and a `Retry-After` header is honored up to 30 seconds. Every attempt has the same request id (see [idempotency](/docs/how-it-works/idempotency)). When the attempts run out the last failure is raised as a typed error, never a raw network error.

</Step>
<Step>

### The error map labels the answer

Each status gets a kind and an outcome, either `definite` (PayPal said so) or `unknown` (the effect cannot be known from here).

</Step>
<Step>

### The capture phase decides

In this order: kill switch, no token, 429, other unknown outcomes, invariant faults, then refusals that name the hold, and finally every other refusal.

</Step>
</Steps>

## How statuses are labelled

| Answer | Kind | Outcome | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 401 | auth expired or auth failed | definite | The request was not accepted at all |
| 404 | not found | definite | PayPal does not know the resource |
| 400 | invalid request | definite | A bug or a bad configuration |
| 429 | rate limited | unknown in the map, handled as not run by the saga | See below |
| 5xx | server error | unknown | The capture may have been taken |
| 408, 409, 425 | rejected | unknown | A timeout or an in-flight conflict proves nothing |
| 422 with a known issue | declined, expired, already voided, already captured | definite | Names this hold |
| 422 with an unknown issue, other 4xx | rejected | definite | Not known to name this hold |
| network failure, timeout | network | unknown | No answer exists |

The 422 issue names other than `PREVIOUSLY_CAPTURED` are UNVERIFIED on the sandbox (gate G1). The saga is built so a wrong name is harmless: it always reads the truth before it falls back.

## What changed on 7 Nov 2026

<Accordions>
<Accordion title="A 429 is not an unknown outcome (PR 1938)">

Before this change a capture that kept getting 429 was treated like a timeout: the ten minute unknown clock started, and a restart after a long outage could push the settlement into review. A 429 means PayPal declined to run the request, so now it starts no clock. The capture waits as `rate_limited`, keeps its intent, and the next pass reads the truth first and repeats the same request id. A 429 that follows an earlier ambiguous attempt leaves that attempt's clock as it was.

Issue: https://github.com/harshbhx/gavel-auction/issues/1937. Fix: https://github.com/harshbhx/gavel-auction/pull/1938.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="A refusal after a 429 stall is still a refusal (PR 1947)">

A capture that PayPal refuses because the authorization is older than its honor period names this hold, not the merchant. After a 429 stall such a refusal, and a declined or expired one, is classified like a first refusal: the truth is read and the settlement falls back to the runner-up at once, instead of waiting for the ten minute deadline. The honor-period issue name `AUTHORIZATION_NOT_HONORED_AFTER_HONOR_PERIOD` is UNVERIFIED (gate G3).

Issue: https://github.com/harshbhx/gavel-auction/issues/1946. Fix: https://github.com/harshbhx/gavel-auction/pull/1947.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The soak fails on any 429-caused review (PR 1945)">

The soak test now fails if any settlement reaches review because of a 429. https://github.com/harshbhx/gavel-auction/pull/1945

</Accordion>
</Accordions>

While the kill switch is engaged the unknown clock stands still; the time it held the clock is not counted afterwards.

<Callout type="info" title="Why never the next bidder on a maybe">

If the first capture did happen and the runner-up is charged as well, two people paid for one lot. That is the one mistake the saga must not make, so every maybe waits or goes to a person.

</Callout>

## What is proven

<NotVerified title="Fake PayPal and simulation only">

The behaviour is tested against the fake PayPal, including the 429 cases in `packages/payments/test/saga/rateLimited.test.ts`, and in the simulated 30-day run, where eight lots get two injected 429s on their first capture and a restart after a two hour outage. The fake showed 0 answers with 429 in 60 cycles; PayPal's real rate limits are UNVERIFIED (gate G1 measurement), so how often a real run would meet a 429 is not known. See [the simulation](/docs/proof/simulation).

</NotVerified>
