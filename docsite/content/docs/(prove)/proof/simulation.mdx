## What it is

The <Term id="saga">settlement saga</Term>, the <Term id="reauthorization">reauthorization</Term> pass, the <Term id="journal">journal</Term> stores and the <Term id="webhook">webhook</Term> merge run over <Term id="simulated">simulated</Term> days against a **model** of PayPal's authorization rules, in seconds, with an injected clock. The model is the <Term id="fake-paypal">fake PayPal</Term> with these assumptions: <Term id="honor-period">honor period</Term> 3 days, authorization valid 29 days, reauthorize allowed only after the honor period and answered with a new authorization id, capture after validity fails.

<NotVerified title="What this proves, and what it does not">

A passing run says our code behaves correctly **if PayPal follows this model**. It says nothing about whether PayPal does. That is gate <Term id="gate-g3">G3</Term>, a real probe that is still in progress. Where G3 disagrees with the model, the model changes and the suite reruns.

</NotVerified>

## S8: thirty days, a hundred bidders, random crashes

Forty lots, 100 bidders, a 3 percent crash chance at every boundary, outages of 1 to 4 days in 5 percent of crashes, and random PayPal faults. Every fifth lot gets two injected rate-limit (429) answers on its first capture and a restart after a two hour outage. The figures on the timeline above are read from the committed file.

Process crashes in this suite are exceptions at a saga boundary: they are not real process kills.
