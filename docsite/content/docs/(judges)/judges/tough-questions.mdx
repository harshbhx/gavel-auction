<Accordion title="How accurate is voice bidding, and why does every spoken bid still confirm?">

Gate G5 is PARTIAL: voice bidding is measured on synthetic audio, and live-room confirmation is the next step. After the parser fixes and the provider options (Deepgram nova-3, Groq whisper-large-v3-turbo with a bid prompt), wrong accept is 0.000 and exact plus recoverable is 1.000 on all four runs: 24 synthetic eSpeak clips and 24 human-like synthetic clips, against a limit of 0.05.

The first run, on Deepgram nova-2 and Groq without a prompt, scored a wrong-accept rate of 0.125 and 0.083 on the eSpeak set and 0.083 and 0.167 on the second set. Nine of the eleven wrong accepts were a label defect in our own evaluation; the parser and provider options fixed the rest. Those fixes were chosen on the same clips, the clips are noise-free, and no real-room or noisy-room run exists yet, so the numbers are a strong start and not a pass of the gate question.

The decision recorded in `docs/GATES.md` is that spoken bids always go through the <Term id="confirm-prompt">confirm prompt</Term>. The parser never decides a winner and never moves money; typed entry and QR bids are the other input paths, so the auction works if speech fails. At the text level (transcripts through the parser, no audio) wrong accepts are 0.000 on 169 cases, which measures the parser only.

See [voice and the confirm gate](/docs/how-it-works/voice-and-confirm).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="When does gate G3 report, and what if a hold expires?">

G3 is a real timed probe on the sandbox. Day 0 was 7 Nov 2026 03:06 UTC and the day 0 checks are recorded: an immediate reauthorize was refused with `REAUTHORIZATION_TOO_SOON`, and three authorizations were approved to be read at 3.5, 4 and 5 days. The final reading is about 12 Nov 06:17 UTC. Earlier attempts on 4 to 7 Nov never ran, so no earlier day is claimed. Until the readings exist the gate reads NOT RUN / IN PROGRESS.

An early reading on an older G2 wallet authorization, taken on 8 Nov 17:36 UTC at age 3.24 days, accepted a partial capture of 1.00 (HTTP 201) and answered the reauthorize call with 422 `UNPROCESSABLE_ENTITY` and the issue `PAYER_CANNOT_PAY`. That authorization already carried a partial capture, so it is an early data point; the day 3.5, 4 and 5 authorizations give the clean answer.

PayPal's documentation names an <Term id="honor-period">honor period</Term> (3 days) and a validity window (29 days, with one <Term id="reauthorization">reauthorization</Term>), and GAVEL does not assume either until the probe reports. The plan is to capture within the verified window and reauthorize before the hammer. That is a plan, not a result. Meanwhile holds older than two hours at settlement are flagged stale, and a stale hold is still captured.

The 30-day run is a simulation on a fake clock against a model of PayPal. It shows our code is consistent with that model.

See [honor period](/docs/how-it-works/honor-period) and [gate G3](/docs/proof/gate-g3).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Is any of this real PayPal, or is it all the fake?">

Both exist and they are labelled. Real on the PayPal <Term id="sandbox">sandbox</Term>: G0 (Actions run log only), G1 (authorize 500, capture 350 with final capture, two PayPal-account buyers, one funded by a card), G2 (wallet and guest card authorize), G4 (six real webhooks verified) and one lot of three holds plus one in-process crash in the nightly specs R7, R8 and R10.

Fake: whole-auction settlement with many holds, crash and resume, and rejection of a tampered webhook. The <Term id="fake-paypal">fake PayPal</Term> is a test double in `packages/payments/src/fake/`; it says nothing about the real PayPal.

Never real: money. Every PayPal call is against the sandbox.

See [real, simulated, recorded](/docs/judges/real-vs-simulated).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Is the remainder of a partial capture really released?">

Inferred from the evidence. After the final capture the authorization reads CAPTURED and an explicit void answers 422 `PREVIOUSLY_CAPTURED`. Reading a buyer balance, and capturing a guest card authorization (G1 used PayPal-account orders), are the next steps.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What stands between this sandbox system and production?">

GAVEL runs in sandbox mode today, and the next steps are listed on [Limits and next steps](/docs/roadmap/limits). In short:

- The hosted app (https://gavel.dualmindlab.tech, sandbox mode) is live with key-less judge rooms; a recorded walk of the hosted flow is the next step. The fake-PayPal run is the local path.
- The file stores run on one instance with a persistent disk.
- Latency: hammer to settled has <Term id="p95">p95</Term> 5591.30 ms, inside the 6 s target, and hammer to captured has p95 3010.04 ms, tuning toward the 2.5 s target (n=20, 6 holds, sandbox). The 5 Nov per-call numbers (n=10) have no committed raw log; the settlement run has a redacted copy.
- Whole-auction settlement through a deployed server and a real process kill on the sandbox are the next sandbox runs.
- Rejected bids are kept in the room log, and the ledger holds accepted bids.
- An audio sender from the web console to the `/audio` socket is next.
- Accessibility: automated checks run; the manual screen reader and zoom pass is next.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Where does the security review stand?">

`docs/SECURITY-REVIEW.md` lists SEC-01 to SEC-18 plus SEC-19 and SEC-20, added for judge rooms and receipt links. All eight P0 items (SEC-01 to SEC-07 and SEC-18) are PASS as of 2026-11-07, reviewed by someone other than the author. Of the 18 original items, 16 are PASS, one (SEC-11, audio and personal data) is ACCEPTED with its justification, and one (SEC-15, the log scan) is open. Automated checks cover access control, webhook authenticity and SSRF, the sandbox-only guard, abusive input, headers and denial of service in `packages/e2e/test/security`, and CI runs a secret scan and a dependency audit.

See [security review](/docs/safety/security-review) and the [threat model](/docs/safety/threat-model).

<Accordion title="How do I know the audit trail was not edited?">

Accepted bids go into a <Term id="hash-chain">hash-chained</Term> <Term id="ledger">ledger</Term>. A public export can be checked with `verifyPublicExport` or the `verify-ledger` command in `packages/engine`; an edited entry fails at its index and a cut chain fails with `truncated` when the expected head is passed. The limit: rejected bids are not in the ledger, so it cannot prove them.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="How solid are the latency numbers?">

They are sandbox numbers from single runs, and they are specific. The end-to-end settlement numbers come from run 42346811713 (20 settlements of 6 holds, 0 failed, a redacted copy committed under `docs/evidence/measure/`): hammer to settled p95 5591.30 ms, inside 6 s, and hammer to captured p95 3010.04 ms, tuning toward 2.5 s. The per-call numbers come from run 42047543821 (10 cycles, 0 failures); its raw log is not committed. A repeat on a larger sample is the next step. See [latency](/docs/proof/latency).
