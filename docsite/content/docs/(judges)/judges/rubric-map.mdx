The PayPal AI Hackathon scores five equally weighted criteria from 1 to 5 in Stage Two (rules section 6, as recorded in the project's hackathon notes). Stage One is pass or fail: a working build, a public repository with a detectable open-source license, a public video under three minutes, and a project that fits the theme and uses PayPal and AI. This page does not score anything. It points each criterion at the pages and files a judge can open, and it says where the evidence is thin.

## The five criteria

### 1. Technological Implementation

Thorough, skilled use of the PayPal platform and AI, a genuine and non-trivial implementation.

| What to open | What it shows | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Gates G0 to G5](/docs/proof/gates) | Real sandbox runs for authorize, partial capture, card and guest authorize and webhook verification | PASS for G0, G1, G2, G4 |
| [Settlement saga](/docs/how-it-works/settlement-saga), [Idempotency](/docs/how-it-works/idempotency) | A pure state machine, a journal saved before each call, deterministic request ids | Tested against the fake PayPal; one lot of three holds and one in-process crash on the real sandbox |
| [Webhooks](/docs/safety/webhooks) | Signature verification through PayPal's endpoint | PASS on six real events (G4); tampered-event rejection is fake only |
| [Voice and the confirm gate](/docs/how-it-works/voice-and-confirm) | Speech-to-text, a parser and a validator that never decides a winner or moves money | G5 PARTIAL, synthetic audio only; every spoken bid confirms |
| [Quality checks](/docs/proof/quality-checks) | Unit, property, scenario and differential checks run in CI | See the page for what is and is not measured |

| What to open | What it shows | Status |
